# CSA cloud discovery starter

## Discovery conversation

Confirm the business objective, desired outcome, decision scope, stakeholders,
constraints, dependencies, and blockers. Define success criteria with measurable
KPIs or acceptance criteria. Capture an owner and next step only when participants
make an explicit commitment.

## Migration and modernization

Common strategies are rehost (lift-and-shift or move as-is), replatform, refactor
or rearchitect, repurchase, retain, and retire. Select a strategy per workload
after assessing architecture, data, identity, networking, compliance, operations,
skills, downtime tolerance, cost, and business value.

Azure examples include Azure Migrate for discovery and migration, Azure App
Service and Azure Kubernetes Service for application platforms, Azure SQL and
Azure Database for PostgreSQL for managed data, Azure Storage, Azure Backup,
Azure Site Recovery, Azure Monitor, Microsoft Defender for Cloud, and Microsoft
Entra ID. Product choice requires validated workload requirements; a related
service name alone is not evidence that it was discussed in a meeting.

## Foundation, governance, and security

A landing zone or cloud foundation establishes identity, management groups,
subscriptions, networking, policy, logging, security, and operating ownership.
Use least privilege, privileged access controls, identity or access governance,
encryption, secrets management, threat protection, and auditable policy. Confirm
data residency, regulatory, privacy, and business-continuity requirements.

## Cost and value

Estimate total cost of ownership (TCO), cloud spend, implementation effort,
engineering effort, operational work, and ongoing support. Relate cost to
business benefits and return on investment (ROI). Record assumptions, demand,
licensing, migration cost, optimization opportunities, and budget constraints.

## Reliability measures

Recovery time objective (RTO) is the targeted time to restore service. Recovery
point objective (RPO) is the tolerated data-loss window. A service-level
agreement (SLA) is a provider or customer commitment; a service-level objective
(SLO) is a reliability target. Validate availability, latency, backup, disaster
recovery, business continuity, testing, and ownership rather than treating these
terms as interchangeable.
